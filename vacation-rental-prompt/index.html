The vacation rental prompt — the full prompt

This is the prompt behind the beach rental comparison in this week’s email — the one that turned a month on the Texas coast, three towns, and a very specific bed layout into one document I could actually choose from. Fill in the brackets with your trip, then paste the whole thing into your AI tool and let it do the comparing.

🏖️ You only need to fill in the brackets: your destinations, your dates, who is coming, the bed layout and the must-haves. The prompt makes it verify every detail on the listing page, price both stay lengths all-in, and link every stay straight to its booking page.

EDIT THESE FIRST — replace every [BRACKET] with your trip: [DESTINATIONS] — the towns you're choosing between, e.g. Port Aransas, North Padre, South Padre [DATES / LENGTHS] — the trip windows, priced as exact lengths, e.g. "21 nights and 28 nights in July — price both lengths, no other numbers" [PARTY] — who's coming and when, e.g. "family of 4 for the whole stay, each set of grandparents for one week each — kids are 4 and 1" [BEDROOMS / BATHS] — e.g. "3 bedrooms / 2 baths, sleeps 6+ — or 2 bedrooms, but only if the second bedroom has a bigger bed (Queen/King) AND a twin in the same room" [MUST-HAVES] — the non-negotiables, e.g. "under a 10-minute walk to the beach; single level ideal (stairs matter for the kids, not the grandparents); pool access; a place where we can drive a golf cart to dinner" [NICE-TO-HAVES] — the things that tip a tie, e.g. "beach view, grill and seating outside, walkable to restaurants and shops" [BUDGET ALL-IN] — the ceiling, all fees included, e.g. "under $12,000 all-in for the stay" [BOOKING SITE] — where you're booking, e.g. VRBO Now do this: 1. Start with my criteria, not with what's cheapest. Build one comparison document for ALL of [DESTINATIONS] together — one document, not a separate essay for each town, and never a town-versus-town argument. I want 6-8 options per destination. 2. For every stay, verify the details on the actual listing page — never trust the search result card alone: - Bedrooms BY FLOOR (which bedrooms are downstairs, which are up — for a family with small kids, this is a deciding factor, not a footnote) - Bed sizes in EVERY bedroom, written out per room — especially the second bedroom: a Queen/King plus a twin is a different stay than a Double plus a twin, and the listing has to say which - The walk to the beach, and read it carefully: booking sites measure walk time to the NEAREST beach point, not necessarily the beach I named. Use the listing's own beach-access wording (steps, gated access, 100 ft) and FLAG it whenever the site's number and the listing's words disagree - Pool: private, community or shared — say which - Grill and outdoor seating: yes or no 3. Price it the way the trip is actually booked: the all-in checkout total for [DATES / LENGTHS] only, fees included, for each length. Always price BOTH lengths — the monthly discount can make a longer stay cheaper than a shorter one, and I want to see that instead of assuming shorter means cheaper. 4. Score every option against [MUST-HAVES] first, then [NICE-TO-HAVES]. A stay that misses a must-have is out, even if it's the cheapest — say so plainly, don't dress it up. For the ones that survive, name the trade-off in one line each: "cheapest, but two-story with the kids' room upstairs" — that kind of honesty is the whole point. 5. In the document, every stay gets a big, full-width button that links STRAIGHT to its booking page — "View on [BOOKING SITE] ↗". The title text alone is not a link. On a phone, every stay must be tappable in one obvious place. 6. Add filters or labels so I can narrow it fast: single level, beach view, grill, under budget, and one for the exact bed layout in [BEDROOMS / BATHS] if I gave one. 7. When I change the brief mid-search (I will — a new layout, a dropped town, a new must-have), rebuild the whole document to the new brief and keep every price current. Don't leave old numbers standing next to new ones. Rules: never present a price you didn't see on a live checkout page today; never call something "under a 10-minute walk" on the site's number alone when the listing says otherwise; if two stays are close in price, recommend the one that fits the kids' ages better, and say why in one sentence.
